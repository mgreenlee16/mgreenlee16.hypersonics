Standard Atmosphere

Properties of the 1976 U.S. Standard Atmosphere from sea level to 86 km, and the flight condition at a given Mach number: velocity, dynamic pressure, total conditions and Reynolds number.

Model

The 1976 U.S. Standard Atmosphere represents the atmosphere up to 86 km as seven layers in which temperature varies linearly with geopotential altitude \(h = r_0z/(r_0+z)\). Pressure follows from hydrostatic balance with the perfect-gas law:

$$\frac{p}{p_b} = \left(\frac{T}{T_b}\right)^{-g_0/(RL)}\ \ (L\ne0), \qquad \frac{p}{p_b} = \exp\left[-\frac{g_0(h-h_b)}{RT_b}\right]\ \ (L=0).$$

Viscosity uses Sutherland's law, \(\mu = 1.458\times10^{-6}\,T^{3/2}/(T+110.4)\). The flight-condition values use the γ set in the top bar for total conditions; the speed of sound uses γ = 1.4. The Pitot reading assumes a normal shock ahead of the probe when M > 1.
