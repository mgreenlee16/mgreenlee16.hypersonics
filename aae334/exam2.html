Part 1Objectives checklist

Tick each item when you can do it on a blank sheet with only the equation sheet and tables. Ticks are saved in this browser.

Finite wings (lectures 13–16, HW 6)

  • Relate the geometric, effective and induced angles of attack in Prandtl's lifting-line equation.
  • Compute CL, αi, downwash, CDi, Di and Γ₀ for an elliptically loaded wing.
  • Given Γ(y) or the Fourier coefficients Aₙ, find the lift, the downwash or induced angle at a station, δ, e and CDi.
  • Explain the effect of aspect ratio on the lift slope and induced drag, and sketch lift curves and drag polars for different AR.
  • Match tapered, swept and twisted planforms to their cℓ(y) and L′(y) distributions; explain tip stall and three ways to mitigate it.
  • Compare the lift slope, stall angle and CL,max of a delta wing and a high-AR wing; explain vortex lift and vortex breakdown.

Longitudinal static stability (HW 7)

  • Build CL and CM,cg for a wing–tail airplane, including downwash, ε₀, iw and it.
  • Find CLα, CMα, the neutral point, the static margin, VH, and the CG for a required static margin.
  • Find the trim condition (CM,cg = 0): trim CL, α, the wing and tail lift coefficients, and the tail incidence.
  • Read a CM,cg–CL (or α) plot: stability, trim, and what changes when the CG, tail size or it change.

Compressible flow (lectures 19–22, HW 8–9)

  • Distinguish adiabatic, reversible and isentropic processes, internal energy and enthalpy; know γ for air and monatomic gases.
  • Use the isentropic relations (p, ρ, T ratios); compute Δs for an ideal gas; find stagnation and sonic conditions.
  • Given T₀, p₀ and M (or p), find T, p, ρ, u, a, h, h₀ anywhere in an isentropic nozzle or tunnel.
  • Use the normal-shock relations (equations or tables) in both directions, and the entropy change across a shock.
  • Change frames to analyze a moving shock or blast wave; find the induced velocity and, for a reflected shock, the new state.
  • Find M and V from a Pitot probe in subsonic flow (isentropic) and supersonic flow (Rayleigh–Pitot), with a thermocouple for T₀.
  • Fill in increase / decrease / same tables for shocks and isentropic nozzle flow, and explain each entry physically.

Part 2Formula summary

This is my own summary of the relations used in this unit, not the official equation sheet. Air: γ = 1.4, R = 287 J/(kg K) = 1716 ft·lbf/(slug·°R), cp = 1004.5 J/(kg K) = 6006 ft·lbf/(slug·°R).

Lifting line and elliptic loading

$$\alpha(y_0) = \frac{\Gamma(y_0)}{\pi V_\infty c(y_0)} + \alpha_{L=0}(y_0) + \frac{1}{4\pi V_\infty}\int_{-b/2}^{b/2}\frac{d\Gamma/dy}{y_0 - y}\,dy$$ $$\text{elliptic: } w = -\frac{\Gamma_0}{2b},\ \alpha_i = \frac{C_L}{\pi AR},\ C_{D_i} = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi AR},\ C_L = \frac{\pi b\Gamma_0}{2V_\infty S}$$

Fourier form

$$\Gamma(\theta) = 2bV_\infty\sum A_n\sin n\theta,\quad y = -\tfrac b2\cos\theta$$ $$C_L = \pi AR\,A_1,\quad \delta = \sum_{n\ge3}n\left(\frac{A_n}{A_1}\right)^2,\quad C_{D_i} = \frac{C_L^2(1+\delta)}{\pi AR},\quad e = \frac{1}{1+\delta}$$ $$\alpha_i(\theta) = \sum nA_n\frac{\sin n\theta}{\sin\theta},\qquad L' = \rho_\infty V_\infty\Gamma$$

Aspect ratio, drag polar

$$a = \frac{a_0}{1 + a_0/(\pi e AR)},\quad C_D = C_{D0} + \frac{C_L^2}{\pi eAR},\quad \left(\frac LD\right)_{\max} = \frac12\sqrt{\frac{\pi eAR}{C_{D0}}}$$ $$\text{Schrenk: } L'(y) = \tfrac12\left[c(y) + \tfrac{4S}{\pi b}\sqrt{1 - (2y/b)^2}\right]\frac{L}{S}$$

Wing–tail airplane

$$C_{L,w} = a_w(\alpha + i_w - \alpha_{0,w}),\quad C_{L,t} = a_t[(1 - \tfrac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha})\alpha + i_t - \epsilon_0],\quad \tfrac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha} = \tfrac{\kappa a_w}{\pi AR_w}$$ $$C_L = C_{L,w} + \tfrac{S_t}{S_w}C_{L,t},\quad C_{M,cg} = C_{M,ac,w} + C_L(\bar x_{cg} - \bar x_{ac}) - V_HC_{L,t},\quad V_H = \tfrac{S_t\ell_t}{S_w\bar c}$$

Stability and trim

$$C_{L\alpha} = a_w + \tfrac{S_t}{S_w}a_t(1 - \tfrac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}),\quad C_{M\alpha} = C_{L\alpha}(\bar x_{cg} - \bar x_{ac}) - V_Ha_t(1 - \tfrac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha})$$ $$\bar x_{np} = \bar x_{ac} + \frac{V_Ha_t(1 - d\epsilon/d\alpha)}{C_{L\alpha}},\quad SM = \bar x_{np} - \bar x_{cg} = -\frac{C_{M\alpha}}{C_{L\alpha}}$$ $$\text{trim: } C_{L,t} = \frac{C_{M,ac,w} + C_L(\bar x_{cg} - \bar x_{ac})}{V_H},\quad C_{L,w} = C_L - \tfrac{S_t}{S_w}C_{L,t}$$

Thermodynamics

$$p = \rho RT,\ h = c_pT,\ c_p - c_v = R,\ \gamma = \tfrac{c_p}{c_v},\ c_p = \tfrac{\gamma R}{\gamma-1}$$ $$s_2 - s_1 = c_p\ln\tfrac{T_2}{T_1} - R\ln\tfrac{p_2}{p_1},\qquad \tfrac{p_2}{p_1} = \left(\tfrac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}\right)^\gamma = \left(\tfrac{T_2}{T_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\ \text{(isentropic)}$$

Isentropic flow

$$\tfrac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2,\quad \tfrac{p_0}{p} = \left(\tfrac{T_0}{T}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}},\quad \tfrac{\rho_0}{\rho} = \left(\tfrac{T_0}{T}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}$$ $$h_0 = h + \tfrac{u^2}{2},\quad \tfrac{T^*}{T_0} = 0.8333,\ \tfrac{p^*}{p_0} = 0.5283,\ \tfrac{\rho^*}{\rho_0} = 0.6339\ (\gamma = 1.4)$$

Normal shock

$$M_2^2 = \frac{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_1^2}{\gamma M_1^2 - \frac{\gamma-1}{2}},\quad \frac{p_2}{p_1} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(M_1^2 - 1),\quad \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2}{2 + (\gamma-1)M_1^2}$$ $$T_{02} = T_{01},\qquad s_2 - s_1 = -R\ln\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}$$

Moving shocks and Pitot probes

$$\text{moving shock at } W\text{: } M_1 = W/a_1,\quad u_{air} = W - M_2a_2 = W(1 - \rho_1/\rho_2)$$ $$\text{subsonic Pitot: } p_0/p = (1 + \tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\ \ (\text{use if } p_0/p < 1.893)$$ $$\frac{p_{02}}{p_1} = \left[\frac{(\gamma+1)^2M_1^2}{4\gamma M_1^2 - 2(\gamma-1)}\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\frac{1 - \gamma + 2\gamma M_1^2}{\gamma+1}$$

Part 4Mistakes that cost points

  • Reference area. The airplane lift coefficient is CL = L/(q∞Sw), based on the wing area only. Do not add St to Sw.
  • Airfoil versus wing lift slope. In stability problems, use the finite-wing slopes aw = a₀/(1 + a₀/(πAR)) and at, never dcℓ/dα of the airfoil.
  • Trim means CM,cg = 0. To split the lift between wing and tail at trim, impose CM,cg = 0 together with CL = CL,w + (St/Sw)CL,t. Assuming iw = it = 0 instead of solving for them loses credit.
  • Downwash. The tail sees α(1 − dε/dα) − ε₀ + it; keep ε₀ when the wing has camber or incidence.
  • Supersonic Pitot probes. If pPitot/p > 1.893 a normal shock stands ahead of the probe: use the Rayleigh–Pitot relation (or p₀₂/p₁ in the shock table), not the isentropic relation.
  • Freestream versus measured p₀. Behind the shock the probe reads p₀₂ < p₀∞; T₀ is the same on both sides.
  • Frames for moving shocks. The jump relations hold in the shock frame. Convert back: the air behind a blast wave moves at W − u₂ in the direction of the wave.
  • Tables. Use the closest entry, and say which table and entry you used. Keep 4 significant digits.
  • Absolute temperature. Convert °C to K and °F to °R before using p = ρRT or a = √(γRT).
  • Multiple choice. Erase completely; two marked answers are graded wrong. For "circle all that apply", every choice must be right.

Part 9How to study for it

  • Practice like you play. Cover a solution and rework the problem on blank paper using only the equation sheet and tables; then check. The practice exam's timer and table rule are there for this.
  • Retrieval practice. Answer without notes: describe a wing that has induced drag at CL = 0, and why; sketch delta-wing and high-AR lift curves on one plot; explain what the tail volume coefficient does for stability.
  • Elaboration. Explain in your own words the minimum requirements for longitudinal static stability, and the advantages and disadvantages of tapering a wing.
  • Make a one-pager. Summarize the unit on a single sheet of unlined paper with colors and sketches; arrange it on purpose so the relationships are visible.
  • Redo the homework (HW 6–9) and the previous semester's exam under timed conditions.
