Longitudinal Static Stability

An airplane in steady flight must do two things in pitch: balance (trim), so the moment about its center of gravity is zero, and recover, so that a gust that pitches the nose up produces a nose-down moment. This lesson builds the moment equation of a wing–tail airplane from the finite-wing results of Lessons 1–3 and derives the neutral point, the static margin and the trim conditions.

Section 5.1Trim and static stability

An airplane is trimmed (in equilibrium) when the sum of moments about its center of gravity is zero, \(C_{M,cg} = 0\). It is statically stable if, when disturbed from equilibrium, the initial tendency is to return: a nose-up disturbance (α increases) must create a nose-down moment (CM,cg becomes negative). Static stability is about that initial tendency only; whether the motion then oscillates and dies out is dynamic stability, a later course.

Section 5.2The CM,cg–α line

In the linear range the moment coefficient about the CG is a straight line in the absolute angle of attack αa (measured from the airplane's zero-lift line) or, equivalently, in CL:

$$C_{M,cg} = C_{M,0} + C_{M\alpha}\,\alpha_a = C_{M,0} + \frac{dC_M}{dC_L}C_L,\qquad C_{M,0} \equiv C_{M,cg}\big|_{C_L = 0}.$$

Two requirements follow, and they are the heart of the topic:

  • Stability: \(C_{M\alpha} = \partial C_{M,cg}/\partial\alpha_a < 0\) (the line slopes down).
  • Trim at positive lift: with a negative slope, the line crosses CM = 0 at a positive αa only if \(C_{M,0} > 0\).

The trim point is \(\alpha_{a,\text{trim}} = -C_{M,0}/C_{M\alpha}\), \(C_{L,\text{trim}} = C_{L\alpha}\alpha_{a,\text{trim}}\).

Section 5.3The wing alone

Take moments about the CG of the wing's lift (acting at its aerodynamic center) and its moment about the AC. With x̄ = x/c̄ measured aft from the leading edge of the mean aerodynamic chord, and small angles,

$$C_{M,cg} = c_{m,ac} + C_{L,w}\left(\bar x_{cg} - \bar x_{ac}\right).$$

So \(C_{M\alpha} = a_w(\bar x_{cg} - \bar x_{ac})\), negative (stable) only if the CG is ahead of the aerodynamic center, and \(C_{M,0} = c_{m,ac}\). A positively cambered airfoil has \(c_{m,ac} < 0\), so a conventional wing alone that is stable cannot be trimmed at positive lift. Flying wings solve this with a reflexed airfoil (\(c_{m,ac} \ge 0\)) or a swept wing with washout, whose nose-down tips are behind the CG and give a positive CM,0 contribution. Everyone else adds a horizontal tail.

Section 5.4Adding a tail: downwash and tail volume

Let α be the angle of the fuselage reference line (FRL), iw and it the incidence angles of the wing and tail relative to it, and α0w the wing's zero-lift angle. The wing works at \(\alpha + i_w - \alpha_{0w}\) from its zero-lift line:

$$C_{L,w} = a_w(\alpha + i_w - \alpha_{0w}),\qquad a_w = \frac{a_0}{1 + a_0/(\pi AR_w)}\ \ (\text{elliptic}).$$

The wing's trailing vortices push air down at the tail. The downwash angle is proportional to the wing lift, and for an elliptic wing it is about twice the induced angle at the wing (far downstream the induced velocity of a semi-infinite vortex doubles):

$$\varepsilon = \kappa\frac{C_{L,w}}{\pi AR_w} = \varepsilon_0 + \frac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha}\alpha,\qquad \frac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha} = \frac{\kappa a_w}{\pi AR_w},\quad \kappa \approx 2,\qquad \varepsilon_0 = \frac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha}(i_w - \alpha_{0w}).$$

The tail (symmetric airfoil, slope at) sees \(\alpha_t = \alpha - \varepsilon + i_t\), so

$$C_{L,t} = a_t\left[\left(1 - \frac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha}\right)\alpha + i_t - \varepsilon_0\right].$$

Only the fraction \(1 - d\varepsilon/d\alpha\) of each change in α reaches the tail. With the tail's lift based on its own area St and the same dynamic pressure as the wing, the airplane lift coefficient (based on Sw) is

$$C_L = C_{L,w} + \frac{S_t}{S_w}C_{L,t},\qquad C_{L\alpha} = a_w + \frac{S_t}{S_w}a_t\left(1 - \frac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha}\right).$$

The tail's lift acts at a distance ℓt behind the wing's AC. Its moment, made nondimensional, brings in the horizontal tail volume coefficient

$$V_H = \frac{S_t\,\ell_t}{S_w\,\bar c}.$$

VH measures how powerful the tail is in pitch: area times lever arm. Typical values are 0.3–0.6 for light aircraft and 0.5–1.0 for transports.

Section 5.5The complete moment equation

Sum the wing and tail moments about the CG, with ℓt measured from the wing's AC (tail drag and the fuselage's own moment neglected):

$$\boxed{\;C_{M,cg} = c_{m,ac} + C_L\left(\bar x_{cg} - \bar x_{ac}\right) - V_H\,C_{L,t}\;}$$

Differentiate with respect to α:

$$C_{M\alpha} = C_{L\alpha}\left(\bar x_{cg} - \bar x_{ac}\right) - V_H\,a_t\left(1 - \frac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha}\right).$$

The first term is the wing's (destabilizing if the CG is behind the AC); the second is the tail's, always stabilizing. The value at zero airplane lift is

$$C_{M,0} = c_{m,ac} - V_H\,C_{L,t}\big|_{C_L = 0}.$$

At zero airplane lift the tail usually carries a download (negative CL,t, from a negative it or the wing's camber), which makes CM,0 positive. Neglecting the tail's small share of the total lift, \(C_{M,0} \approx c_{m,ac} - V_Ha_t(i_t - i_w + \alpha_{0w})\): making it more negative raises CM,0.

Section 5.6Neutral point and static margin

Moving the CG aft makes CMα less negative. The neutral point is the CG location at which \(C_{M\alpha} = 0\):

$$\bar x_{np} = \bar x_{ac} + V_H\frac{a_t}{C_{L\alpha}}\left(1 - \frac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha}\right).$$

It is the aerodynamic center of the whole airplane, a property of the configuration that does not move with the CG. Substituting back,

$$C_{M\alpha} = -C_{L\alpha}\left(\bar x_{np} - \bar x_{cg}\right),\qquad \text{SM} = \bar x_{np} - \bar x_{cg} = -\frac{C_{M\alpha}}{C_{L\alpha}} = -\frac{dC_M}{dC_L}.$$

The static margin must be positive for static stability (CG ahead of the neutral point); typical values are 5–15% of c̄. A large static margin is very stable but sluggish and needs large tail forces (and trim drag) to trim; a small or negative one is responsive but requires the pilot or a flight computer to stabilize the airplane (relaxed-stability fighters).

What changes whatA bigger tail, a longer tail arm, a higher tail lift slope (higher ARt) or less downwash moves the neutral point aft (more SM). Moving the CG aft reduces SM but does not move the neutral point. Changing it or the elevator shifts CM,0 and the trim point but leaves CMα and SM unchanged.

Section 5.7Trim: finding α, CL and it

In steady level flight, two conditions hold at once: lift equals weight, and the moment about the CG is zero. A typical homework/exam problem gives the weight, speed and geometry and asks for the trim condition:

  1. Lift coefficient from L = W: \(C_L = W/(q_\infty S_w)\) (based on the wing area only).
  2. Tail lift for trim from CM,cg = 0: \(C_{L,t} = [c_{m,ac} + C_L(\bar x_{cg} - \bar x_{ac})]/V_H\).
  3. Wing lift: \(C_{L,w} = C_L - (S_t/S_w)C_{L,t}\).
  4. Angle of attack: \(\alpha = C_{L,w}/a_w - i_w + \alpha_{0w}\).
  5. Tail incidence needed: \(i_t = C_{L,t}/a_t - (1 - d\varepsilon/d\alpha)\alpha + \varepsilon_0\).

Or, the other way round, given it: solve CM,cg(α) = 0 for αtrim, then CL,trim = CL(αtrim) and the trim speed \(V = \sqrt{2W/(\rho S_w C_{L,\text{trim}})}\). A more negative it raises CM,0 and so raises CL,trim (slower trim speed); the static margin does not change.

Section 5.8Key equations and procedure

Lift

$$C_{L,w} = a_w(\alpha + i_w - \alpha_{0w}),\quad \frac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha} = \frac{\kappa a_w}{\pi AR_w}$$ $$C_{L,t} = a_t\left[\left(1 - \tfrac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha}\right)\alpha + i_t - \varepsilon_0\right]$$ $$C_L = C_{L,w} + \frac{S_t}{S_w}C_{L,t},\quad C_{L\alpha} = a_w + \frac{S_t}{S_w}a_t\left(1 - \tfrac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha}\right)$$

Moment

$$C_{M,cg} = c_{m,ac} + C_L(\bar x_{cg} - \bar x_{ac}) - V_HC_{L,t},\quad V_H = \frac{S_t\ell_t}{S_w\bar c}$$ $$C_{M\alpha} = C_{L\alpha}(\bar x_{cg} - \bar x_{ac}) - V_Ha_t\left(1 - \tfrac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha}\right)$$ $$\bar x_{np} = \bar x_{ac} + V_H\frac{a_t}{C_{L\alpha}}\left(1 - \tfrac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha}\right),\quad \text{SM} = \bar x_{np} - \bar x_{cg} = -\frac{C_{M\alpha}}{C_{L\alpha}}$$

Stable and trimmable: CM,0 > 0 and CMα < 0 (SM > 0).

Section 5.9Worked example

Worked example

A small UAV: Sw = 0.85 m², c̄ = 0.34 m, ARw = 7, thin cambered airfoil (a₀ = 2π, α0w = −3°, cm,ac = −0.06), iw = 2°, x̄ac = 0.25; horizontal tail St = 0.11 m², ARt = 4, symmetric thin airfoil, ℓt = 1.20 m from wing AC to tail AC; x̄cg = 0.30; κ = 2. Find the airplane lift-curve slope.

Solution

aw = 2π/(1 + 2/7) = 4.887 rad⁻¹; at = 2π/(1 + 2/4) = 4.189 rad⁻¹; dε/dα = 2(4.887)/(7π) = 0.4444; St/Sw = 0.1294.

CLα = 4.887 + 0.1294(4.189)(1 − 0.4444) = 4.887 + 0.301 = 5.188 rad⁻¹.

Find the neutral point and static margin.

Solution

VH = 0.1294(1.20/0.34) = 0.4567. x̄np = 0.25 + 0.4567(4.189/5.188)(0.5556) = 0.25 + 0.2049 = 0.4549. SM = 0.4549 − 0.30 = 0.155 (15.5% of c̄).

Is the airplane longitudinally statically stable?

Solution

Yes. SM > 0, i.e. CMα = −CLα·SM = −0.803 rad⁻¹ < 0: a nose-up disturbance produces a nose-down restoring moment. (It is also trimmable at positive lift if CM,0 > 0; see (d).)

Find CL,trim and the trim angle of the FRL for it = 0 and for it = −2°.

Solution

ε₀ = 0.4444(2° + 3°) = 2.22°. Write CM,cg(α) = cm,ac + CL(α)(0.05) − VHCL,t(α), which is linear in α, and set it to zero.

it = 0: αtrim = 2.46°, CL,trim = 0.628 (CL,w = 0.636, CL,t = −0.063: a small download).

it = −2°: αtrim = 7.15°, CL,trim = 1.034 (CL,t = −0.018).

Check one value: at it = 0 and α = 2.46° = 0.04291 rad, CL,w = 4.887(0.04291 + 0.08727) = 0.636; CL,t = 4.189[0.5556(0.04291) − 0.03879] = −0.0626; CM = −0.06 + 0.628(0.05) + 0.4567(0.0626) = 0.000 ✓.

Explain how the tail incidence affects the trim lift coefficient and the static margin.

Solution

Making it more negative increases the tail download at a given α, which raises CM,0 and moves the trim point to a higher α and CL (0.628 → 1.034), i.e. a lower trim speed. Because it enters CM only as a constant, the slope CMα, the neutral point and the static margin (0.155) are unchanged.

Section 5.10Check your understanding

For a longitudinally stable airplane, should CM at CL = 0 be positive, negative or zero? What about CMα?

Answer

CM,0 > 0 (so it can trim at positive lift) and CMα < 0 (stability).

What is the tail volume coefficient and how is it related to longitudinal stability?

Answer

VH = Stℓt/(Swc̄), the tail's area times its moment arm, nondimensionalized. The tail's stabilizing contribution to CMα is −VHat(1 − dε/dα), so a larger VH moves the neutral point aft and increases the static margin.

An airfoil has αL=0 = −2°. What is its cl at α = 6° (thin-airfoil theory)? Is cm,ac likely positive or negative? Would a wing of this airfoil alone be stable and trimmable?

Answer

cl = 2π(8°·π/180) = 0.877. αL=0 < 0 means positive camber, so cm,ac is negative. Alone, with the CG ahead of the AC, it is stable but CM,0 = cm,ac < 0, so it cannot trim at positive lift; it needs a tail, a reflexed airfoil, or sweep with washout.

Explain the minimum requirements for longitudinal static stability, and the role of the static margin.

Answer

CMα < 0, equivalently the CG ahead of the neutral point (SM > 0); plus CM,0 > 0 to trim at positive lift. The SM measures how strongly the airplane resists pitch disturbances: SM = −dCM/dCL.
