About this site

Learn Hypersonics is a self-study site for compressible aerodynamics, hypersonic flight and high-speed testing, built by Michael Greenlee, an aerospace engineering student at Purdue University working on hypersonic boundary-layer transition at the Boeing/AFOSR Mach-6 Quiet Tunnel. It is organized so that someone interested in spaceflight and hypersonics can work from first principles up to re-entry, scramjet propulsion and wind-tunnel testing. The format (short derivations paired with live, adjustable figures, plus calculators that replace the tables) was inspired by Kursat Kara's open Fundamentals of Aerodynamics course site.

How it is built

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step, so it runs straight from GitHub Pages. All physics lives in one file, assets/js/gasdyn.js: the isentropic, normal-shock, oblique-shock, Prandtl–Meyer, Rayleigh and Fanno relations, shock-expansion theory, compressibility corrections, the 1976 U.S. Standard Atmosphere, and supersonic wind-tunnel models (starting, blowdown run time, condensation). The hypersonic material lives in assets/js/hyper.js: Newtonian and hypersonic small-disturbance theory, Taylor–Maccoll cones, Newtonian panel integration of bodies of revolution, 11-species equilibrium air from NASA Glenn polynomials, equilibrium normal shocks, Sutton–Graves and reference-temperature heating, entry trajectories, Brayton-cycle ramjet and scramjet analysis, and shock-tube, Ludwieg-tube and liquefaction models. Lessons and calculators call the same functions. Equations are typeset with KaTeX.

Accuracy

The functions reproduce the standard tables. For M = 2 and γ = 1.4: p₀/p = 7.824, A/A* = 1.688, normal-shock p₀₂/p₀₁ = 0.7209, a θ = 10° wedge gives β = 39.31° (weak) or 83.70° (strong), θmax = 22.97°, ν = 26.38°, Fanno 4fL*/D = 0.3050, and Rayleigh T₀/T₀* = 0.7934. Inverse problems are solved by bisection to near machine precision.

For the hypersonic models: the Taylor–Maccoll solver gives β = 31.2° for a 10° cone at Mach 2 and a maximum cone angle of 57.6° in the hypersonic limit; Newtonian panel integration reproduces CD = 1 for a sphere and the closed-form cone forces at incidence; the equilibrium-air solver agrees with Cantera to a few parts per million; and the equilibrium normal shock at 11 km/s and 52 km gives T₂ ≈ 11,700 K against about 58,800 K for a perfect gas, as in Anderson. Run node tests/validate.js to repeat these checks. Correlations (Sutton–Graves, the reference-temperature method, Millikan–White, Billig, the second-mode frequency estimate) are only as accurate as the correlations themselves, typically 10–20%, and the entry and engine presets are illustrative, not flight reconstructions.
