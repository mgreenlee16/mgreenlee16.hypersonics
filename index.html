Learn Hypersonics

Interactive lessons and calculators that run from the thermodynamics of a perfect gas, through shocks, nozzles and supersonic wind tunnels, to hypersonic flight: Newtonian aerodynamics, high-temperature air, aeroheating and transition, atmospheric entry, scramjet propulsion and hypersonic ground testing. Every figure is computed live in your browser from the governing equations. Change the Mach number, the altitude or the geometry and the results update at once.

Built by Michael Greenlee, aerospace engineering student at Purdue, working on hypersonic boundary-layer transition at the BAM6QT.

Lessons

The lessons build on one another. Part I covers one-dimensional flow, Part II two-dimensional supersonic waves, Part III compressible airfoils, and Part IV supersonic wind tunnels. Parts V–VII turn to hypersonics: what makes the regime different, Newtonian aerodynamics, high-temperature gas dynamics and aeroheating (V); atmospheric entry and air-breathing propulsion (VI); and hypersonic ground testing (VII).

Notes

Using the site

The ratio of specific heats, γ, in the top bar of each lesson and calculator applies to every figure and value on that page, and is remembered between pages. Theme and text size are remembered too.

Lessons 1–10 assume a calorically perfect gas (constant γ and cp), accurate for air up to stagnation temperatures of roughly 800–1,000 K. Lessons 11–15 add equilibrium air (11 species, NASA Glenn thermodynamic data), and say where a model is an engineering correlation (heating, transition, cycle analysis) rather than an exact result. The physics is checked against published values by tests/validate.js. Corrections and suggestions are welcome through the repository's issue tracker.
